DESCRIPTION
===========
OWL FLY II - a new flight simulator for DOS. Not a port and not a remaster -
written now, in 8086 assembly, for the machine you already have.

MIT, and it builds with the assembler that ships beside it. Nothing to buy,
nothing to install.

Fly it in your browser:
https://kirindenis.github.io/wire-city-2/owlfly2.html

And you are not alone up there. Multiplayer over IPX - in your browser, or on
your own DOS box, and everybody ends up in the same sky. You drop in over a
war that is already going on, and Enter puts you in a jet.

Source (MIT), assembler included:
https://github.com/KirinDenis/wire-city-2/tree/main/GAMES/OWLFLY2

Made by Owlos. Keeping old systems alive is our day job.
https://owlos.sk

NOTES
=====
- SHORT ON PURPOSE. This is a gameplay clip, not a lesson: what it is,
  that you can fly it with someone, and that the source is yours. The spec
  sheet belongs in the repository, and anyone who wants it will click.
- The source link is labelled "Source (MIT), assembler included" and not
  "the assembler source": that link goes to the GAME. Anyone in this
  community reading "assembler source" clicks expecting flat assembler's own
  code, finds a flight simulator, and leaves.
- NO MECHANISM in the copy. "Multiplayer over IPX, in your browser or on your
  own DOS box" is the whole of what a viewer needs. ipxnet, the WebSocket
  relay and which packets carry what are answers to comments, not lines in a
  description - these people are here to fly it, not to build it.
- DOS, not MS-DOS, anywhere in the copy. It needs DOS and VGA and does not
  care whose DOS - and half this audience is on FreeDOS.
- The first three hashtags show above the title: #dos #retrogaming #assembly.
  Ten in total; more than fifteen and YouTube ignores every one of them.
- The two QR codes point at the same links, for the end of the clip:
  qr-source.png and qr-play.png. Keep the white margin or phones will not
  read them.
- If somebody asks HOW the multiplayer works: native DOSBoxes find each other
  over ipxnet (NETHOST.BAT / NETJOIN.BAT in the game's folder), and the
  browser build tunnels the same IPX packets through a WebSocket relay, so a
  DOS box and a browser tab share one sky.
- If somebody asks for numbers in the comments: FLYOWL2.EXE is 107,408 bytes
  of 8086 code, plus 162 KB of painted cockpit and recorded sound. It is a
  real MZ executable of five segments - the main one 64K, then SKYSEG, UISEG,
  SYMSEG and TOWNSEG end to end.
- Do not claim multiplayer on bare metal without saying how: IPX needs a
  network stack. Single player is 8086-only by construction, and that is the
  claim worth making.
