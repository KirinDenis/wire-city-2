LESSON 17 — A TOWN ON THE GROUND

click the black screen first to grab the keyboard · this lesson wants a real keyboard · back to the arcade
Type MAKE, then RUN. Space takes off, facing the island's biggest town. Arrows fly it — down is nose up. N is night.

THE TOWN

Blocks of sixteen houses on the flat squares of sand and grass. Nothing is stored: a hash of each square's position decides park or houses, and each house's storeys. The same square always looks the same.

THE WINDOWS

Every pane is drawn exactly as big as it is on the screen, with the perspective corrected. A far window is one lit pixel, and it grows into a pane as you close in. Nothing switches, nothing pops.

THE NIGHT

Press N. Eighty one bytes go to the VGA's DAC and not one pixel is redrawn: every pixel keeps its number, and the numbers mean other colours now.

THE WORLD IS AS BIG AS THE REGISTER

The island is 64 squares of 1024 units: 65,536, exactly one 16-bit word. A house on a square that size would be one house to a hill, and squares twice as big would need a seventeenth bit for every position. So the squares stayed, and everything else — the houses, the storeys, the hills — was halved.

A BYTE OF FRACTION, AND ADC

The aeroplane moves 11 units a frame. Turn one step, and the sideways part of that is 6×11 = 66 two-hundred-and-fifty-sixths of a unit — which a word rounds to nothing, so the aeroplane turns its nose and flies straight on. So a byte of fraction stands beside each coordinate, and the carry out of it goes into the word with the one new instruction of the lesson:

add  [camxf],bl     ; 200 + 100 = 300: the byte keeps 44, CF = 1
adc  [camx],ax      ; camx + the whole units + CF

ADC is ADD plus the carry flag. The trap: nothing between the two may change CF.

WHICH WALLS, AND IN WHAT ORDER

A house shows the walls that face you, decided by four comparisons in the world — west of the west wall, you see the west wall — exactly as OWL FLY III's VISFILL does it. A box never shows three walls, and its walls never cover each other, so nothing inside a house is sorted. The sixteen houses of a square are sorted one axis at a time: the distances along a row of columns make a V, so the farthest is always one of the two ends.

WHAT IT COSTS, MEASURED

DOSBox at 30,000 cycles, retrace wait on, so the rate steps down a whole retrace at a time:

bare ground23 frames a second
over the densest town, no windows17
over the densest town, windows lit14
