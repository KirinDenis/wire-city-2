READING THE GLASS — the SA suite

The scope speaks NATO shapes: friends are squares, hostiles are diamonds, and real pilots wear white with their callsign letter beside the blip. Every blip trails a velocity stub along its own nose; a dot above/below means higher/lower than you. A compass-true grey grid marches under it all, two dots up the radius point north.

Centre MFD: the north-up island map — the red front line, your white blip, humans as a letter plus their altitude digit (thousands). M swaps it for the SIT close-zoom scope or the TGT page (callsign in IFF colour, type, RNG and ALT in K-notation, a live wireframe). Right MFD: the war score with the shape legend, your kills in white, GUN rounds, MSL rails, DAM pips.

In the world, any human in view wears a designator box with a white nameplate — callsign letter and range — at any distance. The AI's IFF boxes light up inside visual range only.

THE WAR

Two squadrons in an attrition war over a procedural island minted from a two-byte seed — which is why joining a sky is instant: the ad carries the seed, your machine builds the same island, city and clouds, and you are there. Buildings fall and stay ruins, wreckage chains, ramming works. Once. You take 5 hits; the fifth replays your last five seconds, then GAME OVER — Space — again.
