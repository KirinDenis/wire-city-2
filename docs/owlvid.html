OWL FLY 4 — video in DOS

640×400, 256 colours, twelve frames a second, with sound. A workbench, not a game — nothing here is finished.

About 23 MB to download before anything happens, and it is loaded in one piece. Give it a moment. Sound needs a click on the picture first, as every browser insists.

What this is

A DOS program playing a video file. Not a recording of one — the decoding is happening in the emulator, in real time, on what amounts to a 486. The picture is assembled a block at a time in system memory and pushed to the card whole; the sound is fed to a Sound Blaster by the DMA controller, and it is the sound that keeps time. Frames are pulled to it. Nothing is timed from a timer.

How it is built

Mode
VESA 100h, 640×400, 256 colours, linear framebuffer
Codec
8×8 blocks — skip, fill, copy, run-length — over a whole-frame motion vector, so a slow pan costs about thirty bytes
Palette
one for the whole clip, ordered dithering only
Sound
8-bit 22 kHz, auto-init DMA, position read from the DMA controller itself
Addressing
unreal mode — a 4 GB limit cached in FS. No DOS extender, nothing licensed, nothing vendored
Built with
FASM, inside DOS. The player is a 4.7 KB .COM file

What is wrong with it

The first third of a second is silence: the sound card's ring buffer starts empty and the picture does not wait for it. The bitrate is roughly a megabyte a second, which is three or four times what a CD of the period could feed — the fix for that is a codebook, and it is not written yet. And eight-bit audio hisses in quiet passages, which is the format doing what eight-bit audio does.
